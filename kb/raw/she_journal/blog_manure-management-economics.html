Part 3 · The Economics of Regenerative Farming

The same tanker of manure can save $20 an acre or cost $34

Use Iowa injection rates and crop-available nutrients to price a manure delivery. See how freight, placement and the fertilizer order change the margin.

SH
By Soil Health Exchange Team|Tuesday, November 17, 2026|6 min read|6 reads
Print / Save PDF

A tanker carries the same pounds out of the barn whichever way it turns. Where it stops determines how many of those pounds replace a fertilizer order. In the example below, the same load and application charge leave one field $19.60/ac ahead and the other $33.60/ac behind. That $53.20 difference starts with two lines on the fertilizer bill.

Run the manure budget with a delivered quote.

Start with the fertilizer bill you can actually reduce

This liquid dairy manure example uses 4,000 gallons per acre and a $0 charge for the manure at the source. The pounds in the table are plant-available amounts that replace planned commercial purchases. Use a current manure analysis, soil test and nutrient plan to set the pounds you can credit and the gallons you can apply. If phosphorus limits the application, run the budget at that lower volume and price the nitrogen or potash you’ll still buy. Penn State Extension on fertilizer replacement value

At Iowa State’s average $18.15 per 1,000 gallons for injection, the field that uses all three nutrients saves $4.90 per 1,000 gallons after application. Multiply by four for the $19.60/ac gain. The application quote can move the field past break-even

Iowa State’s 2026 survey received 16 expected rates for liquid injection. The average was $18.15 and the median $17 per 1,000 gallons. The reported range was $11 to $29, after dropping the lowest and highest responses. Apply those rates to the same full-nutrient budget and the spread between the two ends is $72/ac. Iowa State Custom Rate Survey, pp. 1 and 4

At the average charge, the full-credit field can absorb $4.90 per 1,000 gallons of separate freight. A $5 surcharge uses that up and puts the field $0.40/ac behind. The N-only field is still $5/ac behind with zero separate freight at the reported $11 rate. A rate quote can bundle loading, travel, waiting and application. Ask for the all-in charge for the specific route and volume. Iowa State Extension on manure value and hauling

If the plan caps the application at 2,500 gal/ac, the full-credit field saves $12.25/ac with the average charge and zero separate freight, assuming the same replacement value per gallon and zero minimum job fee. A smaller application can leave more fertilizer to buy for the same crop.

Pay for a change in management when it changes an outcome

If improved placement costs $3 more per 1,000 gallons and the nitrogen you would otherwise buy costs $0.65/lb, the extra operation has to displace about 4.6 more pounds of purchased N per 1,000 gallons to pay through N alone. Minnesota Extension’s first-year planning factors for dairy manure give 55% of total N with sweep injection, 50% with knife or coulter injection, and 20% with broadcast and incorporation after 96 hours. With an illustrative lab result of 27 lb total N per 1,000 gallons, the sweep versus delayed incorporation difference is 9.45 lb of first-year N per 1,000 gallons. If all those pounds reduce the fertilizer order, they are worth about $6.14 per 1,000 gallons, or $24.57/ac at the example volume. Use the factors adopted for your area and plan. University of Minnesota Extension, Table 1

A two-year Minnesota corn trial shows how timing and calibration can change the yield line. In 2018, dragline-injected swine manure applied at the fourth leaf collar produced yields and returns to sidedressing costs comparable to anhydrous ammonia and UAN. In 2019, manure plots received about 45 lb/ac less plant-available N than targeted and had a 6% lower stand than the no-sidedress control. Grain yield was 18% below UAN and 25% below anhydrous. Using the trial’s measured yields and cost assumptions, manure applied within 1.2 miles of the barn returned about $400/ac over sidedressing and related costs, versus $488 for UAN and $545 for anhydrous that year. Verify the N concentration and delivered application rate, and check the stand when putting manure into a growing crop. Pfarr et al., Table 4

Give storage, separation and digestion their own budgets

A separator creates two streams and two routes. Compare the pump hours, electricity, dry matter and nutrient pounds delivered to each destination with your current handling. For compost, price the finished tons after shrink. A buyer pays for delivered material, so count testing, handling and freight before crediting a sale.

A digester brings a longer investment horizon. Include the energy contract, downtime, process power, financing, interconnection and digestate handling. If the original manure already replaces fertilizer, carry that existing value into the baseline. Compare whole-farm cash flows and price changes in fertilizer value separately from changes in hauling and storage. EPA AgSTAR project planning and financing

When two farms share the work, keep a crop budget and a livestock budget. Fertilizer avoided goes on the crop ledger. Storage or handling avoided goes on the livestock ledger. A payment between farms is revenue for one and expense for the other, so it cancels in a combined budget.

Start with two real destinations. Put the soil tests, manure analysis, allowable rates and all-in delivery charges side by side. The field closer to the barn can be the more expensive stop when it has less use for what the tanker carries. Compare nutrient value in the SHE Manure Calculator.
