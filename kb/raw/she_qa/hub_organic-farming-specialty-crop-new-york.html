How much methane does composting a cubic yard of food scraps keep out of the air?

Editorial answer

The number to use for food scraps

EPA's volume-to-weight conversion factors put a cubic yard of food waste at about 463 pounds. EPA's Waste Reduction Model (WARM, version 16) assigns landfilled food waste net emissions of 0.50 metric tons CO2-equivalent (CO2e) per short ton, and composted food waste −0.15, after counting hauling, turning, fugitive emissions and carbon stored in compost-amended soil. The difference is 0.65 metric tons CO2e per ton diverted. A cubic yard of food scraps is 0.23 short tons, so:

  • 1 cubic yard ≈ 0.15 metric tons CO2e avoided (about 330 lb)
  • 10 cubic yards ≈ 1.5 metric tons CO2e avoided
  • 50 cubic yards ≈ 7.5 metric tons CO2e avoided

For volunteers, a familiar comparison helps. EPA estimates a typical passenger vehicle emits about 400 grams of CO2 per mile, so each cubic yard of food scraps composted is roughly equal to 375 miles not driven.

Why food scraps carry most of the benefit

Food breaks down fast once buried, and EPA estimates that about 58% of fugitive methane from municipal landfills comes from landfilled food waste, even though food makes up about 24% of what goes to landfills. In 2019, the US food retail, food service and residential sectors generated 66.2 million tons of wasted food, and only 5% of it was composted.

The credit depends on what is in the pile. WARM gives fruits and vegetables a lower landfill factor (0.23) than mixed food waste, which brings a cubic yard of mostly produce scraps to about 0.09 metric tons CO2e avoided at the same density. For leaves and branches, WARM's landfill factors (−0.53 and −0.54) are lower than its composting factor (−0.05) because it credits landfills with storing undecomposed carbon, so leaves and brush should not be counted toward avoided emissions in this calculation. Grass clippings are the exception: landfilled grass carries a positive factor (0.12). Density varies too: EPA lists leaves at 250 to 500 pounds per cubic yard, and other food waste measurements of 22 to 45 pounds per cubic foot.

Turn it into a running tally for your program

Weigh incoming scraps whenever you can, even with a bathroom scale and a standard bucket, and log food scraps and yard materials separately. Multiply short tons of mixed food scraps by 0.65, or produce-only scraps by 0.38, to get metric tons CO2e avoided. Report the season total alongside a plain statement of the basis, such as: Using EPA WARM factors, the X tons of food scraps our volunteers composted this year instead of sending to landfill avoided about Y metric tons of CO2-equivalent, much of it methane. That keeps the number defensible and gives volunteers a figure that grows with every bucket they bring in, along with finished compost going back onto local soil.
