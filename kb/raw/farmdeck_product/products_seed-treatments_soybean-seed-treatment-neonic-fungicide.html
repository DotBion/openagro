Neonicotinoid + fungicide seed treatment

Syngenta (CruiserMaxx Vibrance Beans), Bayer (Acceleron), others · Soybean

Seed treatmentsConventionalThiamethoxam or imidaclopridBase fungicides

What the research measured

Across 194 trials in 14 states, the cited analysis reported up to 0.04 Mg/ha additional soybean yield from adding a neonicotinoid to a fungicide seed treatment. The sources discuss insecticide and fungicide components separately, with responses depending on pest, disease, and establishment conditions.

Two peer-reviewed multi-state meta-analyses (194 studies in 14 states; 143 studies in 12 states plus 3,614 grower fields), an EPA benefits assessment and Extension syntheses.5 independent sources.

What it is. A factory-applied seed coating pairing a neonicotinoid insecticide with two or more seedling-disease fungicides, protecting the seedling for its first few weeks after planting. Neonicotinoid-treated seed covered a third to nearly half of US soybean acres in the study years.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Sources describe the following risk factors for seedling disease: soils below 60°F at planting, compacted, no-till or flood-prone fields, continuous soybean, seeding rates under 140,000, or a history of seedling disease, SDS or red crown rot.
  • The insecticide is more likely to matter after pasture, grassland or CRP, with heavy decomposing residue, in double-crop soybean, or where bean pod mottle virus is a risk; it is bioactive only 3–4 weeks after planting.
  • Single seasons can show large responses that multi-year averages do not: Iowa State’s 2011 trials found effects at all four sites, for example 71.6 vs 63.3 bu/ac at Crawfordsville.
  • SDS products (ILEVO, Saltro) are separate actives added to the base package and only return money where SDS shows up: an 89% chance of a positive return with severe SDS at $13/ac, very low without symptoms.
  • Economics swing on price: Esker et al. 2026 found a net benefit only at $5.10–8.52 per unit with soybean prices near 20-year highs; a conservative scenario lost $5.89–10.18 per acre.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Neonicotinoid seed treatments of soybean provide negligible benefits to US farmers ↗
    Meta-analysisMourtzinis, Krupke, Esker, Varenhorst, Arneson, Bradley et al. · Scientific Reports (open-access copy at PubMed Central) · 2019doi:10.1038/s41598-019-47442-8

    Pooled 194 randomized, replicated field studies from 14 states (2006–17). Fungicide plus neonicotinoid beat fungicide-only by at most 0.04 Mg/ha; the largest neonicotinoid response (+0.19 Mg/ha) occurred in only 12% of cropping systems. The economic analysis concluded treated seed should not cost more than untreated seed.

  2. Marginal causal effect of fungicide seed treatments on soybean yield and uncertain profitability in the US Midwest ↗
    Meta-analysisEsker, Shah, Mourtzinis, Grassini, Silva & Conley · Scientific Reports (open-access copy at PubMed Central) · 2026doi:10.1038/s41598-026-47390-0

    Fungicide seed treatment alone: 143 rainfed Midwest trials (12 states, 2006–17) averaged 22.2 kg/ha, and 3,614 grower fields in 10 states about 36 kg/ha with confidence intervals overlapping zero. A net benefit appeared only at $5.10–8.52 per unit with soybean prices near 20-year highs.

  3. Benefits of Neonicotinoid Seed Treatments to Soybean Production ↗
    GovernmentUS EPA Office of Pesticide Programs, BEAD · US Environmental Protection Agency · 2014

    EPA concluded neonicotinoid seed treatments provide negligible overall benefit to soybean production in most situations: the treatments are bioactive only 3–4 weeks after planting and, compared with the next-best alternative, likely provide $0 and at most $6 per acre in benefit.

  4. Why Documented Risk Factors Should Guide Soybean Fungicide Seed Treatment Decisions ↗
    ExtensionEsker, Silva, Smith & Conley · Badger Crop Network, UW–Madison Extension · 2026

    In farmer units: fungicide seed treatment averaged 0.33 bu/ac (95% CI 0.01–0.64) in rainfed multi-state trials, about 0.54 bu/ac in grower fields and 1.26 bu/ac in the published literature; a net benefit above $2/ac only at $5.10–7.26 per unit with $11–19 beans, and a conservative scenario returned −$5.89 to −$10.18 per acre.

  5. Seed Treatment and Foliar Fungicide Impact on Sudden Death Syndrome and Soybean Yield ↗
    ExtensionKandel, Mueller, Sisson et al. · Crop Protection Network, CPN-5002 · 2019

    The SDS-targeted active fluopyram (a different active from the neonicotinoid) raised yield 4.4 bu/ac (7.6%) over a base treatment where SDS was severe in multi-state trials, with an 89% chance of a positive return at $13/ac and $10 beans, but a very low chance without symptoms.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What pest or disease pressure did the trials with a response actually have?
  2. What does the treatment cost per unit, and how many bushels would it need to add to pay?
  3. Can I buy the same variety untreated or with a different package?
