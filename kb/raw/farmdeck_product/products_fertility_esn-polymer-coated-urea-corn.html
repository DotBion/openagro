ESN polymer-coated urea

Nutrien (ESN) · Corn

FertilityConventionalPre-plant or at plantingTemperature-controlled release44% N

What the research measured

In 35 Minnesota locations from 2014 to 2018, polymer-coated urea yielded more than urea 26% of the time for corn following corn and 28% for corn following soybean, and never reduced yield relative to urea; fall-applied ESN beat fall urea about 25% of the time. On a sandy loam near Becker, Minnesota (2014–15) the advantage was 39 bu/ac at 120 lb N/ac, while the same treatments on fine-textured southern Minnesota soils showed no difference. A three-year Iowa study found an ESN advantage in one of nine site-years, and a four-year Wisconsin irrigated-sand study averaged 185 bu/ac for pre-plant ESN against 159 for pre-plant urea and 189 for split-sidedressed ammonium sulfate.

Minnesota land-grant trials at 35 locations over five years plus the Becker sandy-loam site, a four-year University of Wisconsin irrigated-sand study (2003–06), and NDSU’s regional summary of Iowa, Nebraska and Michigan trials. The manufacturer’s pages returned 403 and 404 and are not cited.5 independent sources.

What it is. Urea granules sealed in a flexible polymer coating: water diffuses in, dissolves the urea, and the N solution diffuses back out at a rate set by soil temperature and moisture. It carries 44% N against 46% for urea, is broadcast alone or blended with urea before or at planting, and the Wisconsin authors describe release as typically complete about 6 weeks after application.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Soil texture decides much of the response: the 39 bu/ac Becker advantage was on sandy loam, and the same treatments on fine-textured soils in southern Minnesota in 2014 and 2015 showed no yield difference between ESN and urea.
  • Timing matters: Minnesota found a consistent corn yield increase and reduced N loss only with ESN in the pre-plant application; fall ESN out-yielded fall urea about 25% of the time and, averaged over the study, yielded less than spring pre-plant urea.
  • Release is temperature-driven: ESN held at a constant 59°F for 40 days released only 29% of its N.
  • In Wisconsin’s heavy-leaching 2004 season, split-sidedressed ammonium sulfate yielded 196 bu/ac against 167 for pre-plant ESN; the authors conclude that relying on pre-plant ESN as a general practice carries risk of reduced performance in wet years.
  • Other Iowa work (2013–14, two locations) found no agronomic or N-use-efficiency benefit from ESN compared with urea, and Michigan blend-ratio trials showed few corn yield differences among ESN:urea ratios.
  • With low loss potential there is little to protect: the first year of Minnesota’s new study (the 2021 drought) showed little or no difference between treatments. Damaged coatings release immediately; Minnesota suggests soaking 100 granules in room-temperature water for 24 hours, and if five dissolve roughly 5% of the product is damaged.

A seven-year southwest Minnesota study found split application no better than pre-plant.

In the wet 2004 season pre-plant ESN yielded 167 against 141 for pre-plant urea and 196 for split ammonium sulfate.

  3. How does the price per pound of nutrient compare with the sources I already use?
