Nitrapyrin nitrification inhibitor

Corteva Agriscience (N-Serve, Instinct NXTGEN) · Corn

FertilityConventionalWith anhydrous, UAN or manureAbout $13/ac with anhydrous

What the research measured

The cited independent review reported an average yield response of 3.3% to nitrapyrin; a manufacturer-authored review reported 7%. The studies also measured nitrogen losses. Responses differed with nitrogen timing, drainage, soil texture, and season.

Two meta-analyses (one manufacturer-authored, one from Purdue), an independent literature review, 16 paired Illinois on-farm trials, seven Minnesota study years and a six-year Iowa drainage study.4 independent sources · 1 manufacturer-authored, labelled.

What it is. An additive for anhydrous ammonia (N-Serve) or for UAN, urea and liquid manure (Instinct NXTGEN) that temporarily slows the soil bacteria converting ammonium into nitrate, the form that leaches and denitrifies.

Study conditions and limitations
  • In a comparison with fall-applied anhydrous ammonia: Minnesota’s seven study years showed +15 bu/ac for fall anhydrous with N-Serve over fall anhydrous alone, but spring anhydrous beat fall anhydrous alone by 27 bu/ac, in that study.
  • Apply fall ammonia only after soils drop below 50°F and keep cooling; nitrapyrin breaks down faster in warm soil.
  • With spring or sidedress N, three Indiana site-years showed no yield effect, and the NDSU summary reports no yield increase from the encapsulated Instinct product in Illinois, Iowa, Minnesota or Nebraska (2008–09).
  • With liquid swine manure, 11 Iowa on-farm trials responded only in an extremely wet year; profit required spring–summer rain more than 40% above the long-term average.
  • In 16 Illinois paired trials, fall anhydrous with N-Serve matched spring N yield (235 vs 236 bu/ac) but needed about 12 lb/ac more N at the optimum, a $9/ac edge for spring application.
  • The 7% yield, 28% retention, 16% leaching and 51% greenhouse-gas figures quoted in marketing come from a 2004 meta-evaluation whose author worked for the manufacturer; an independent review put the corn yield gain at 3.3%.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Instinct II and N-Serve Research Findings ↗
    Independent reviewEagle (Duke University), review commissioned by the Environmental Defense Fund · NutrientStar tool-finder research review · 2016

    An independent literature review found average corn yield gains of 3.3% (±1.5) with nitrapyrin, just over 2% with anhydrous ammonia and almost 6% with urea, and a 13.1% (±9.3, n = 18) reduction in yield-scaled nitrate loss with anhydrous.

  2. Nitrapyrin Impacts on Maize Yield and Nitrogen Use Efficiency with Spring-Applied Nitrogen: Field Studies vs. Meta-Analysis Comparison ↗
    Meta-analysisBurzaco, Ciampitti & Vyn (Purdue / Kansas State) · Agronomy Journal 106 · 2014doi:10.2134/agronj2013.0043

    In three Indiana site-years with spring-applied N, nitrapyrin improved N recovery efficiency but did not significantly change grain yield; the accompanying meta-analysis found positive yield effects that did not carry through to N-use efficiency. Benefits with spring N are not always realized.

  3. Managing Nitrogen for Corn in 2020 ↗
    University trialNafziger · farmdoc daily, University of Illinois · 2020

    Across 16 paired Illinois on-farm trials, fall anhydrous with N-Serve and spring-applied N produced virtually identical yields (235 vs 236 bu/ac), but the fall system needed about 12 lb/ac more N at the optimum, a $9/ac advantage for spring application.

  4. Nitrogen Extenders and Additives for Field Crops ↗
    ExtensionFranzen · North Dakota State University Extension, SF1581 · 2022

    Seven Minnesota study years: fall anhydrous plus N-Serve yielded 15 bu/ac more than fall anhydrous alone, while spring anhydrous yielded 27 bu/ac more. The encapsulated Instinct formulation showed no yield increase in Illinois, Iowa, Minnesota or Nebraska (2008–09); N-Serve gains depend on rain causing leaching or denitrification.

  5. A meta-evaluation of nitrapyrin agronomic and environmental effectiveness with emphasis on corn production in the Midwestern USA ↗
    ManufacturerWolt (Dow AgroSciences) · Nutrient Cycling in Agroecosystems 69; manufacturer-authored · 2004doi:10.1023/B:FRES.0000025287.52565.99

    Pooling trials across many environments, nitrapyrin raised yield 7%, soil N retention 28%, and cut N leaching 16% and greenhouse-gas emissions 51%. These are the figures the manufacturer quotes; the author was employed by Dow AgroSciences.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What did my soil test show, and did the cited studies respond at similar levels?
  2. What rate, source, and timing were tested, and what did the untreated check receive?
  3. How does the price per pound of nutrient compare with the sources I already use?
