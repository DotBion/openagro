PROVEN 40

Pivot Bio · Corn

BiologicalsConventionalIn-furrow or on-seedMarketed as 40 lb N/acNot OMRI-listed

What the research measured

Published results vary by site and nitrogen rate. The ten-state review reported significant yield increases in 0 of 15 PROVEN 40 site-years; Wisconsin reported none in five trials. The Illinois study reported a mean increase of about 1.8 bu/ac across four site-years. Kentucky and manufacturer studies report additional responses under their tested conditions.

Multi-year, multi-site independent trials: a ten-state land-grant review, five Wisconsin sites, Nebraska on-farm research and a peer-reviewed four-site-year Illinois study.4 independent sources · 1 manufacturer-authored, labelled.

What it is. Gene-edited nitrogen-fixing bacteria (Klebsiella variicola and Kosakonia sacchari) applied in-furrow at 12.8 oz/ac or on the seed, marketed as supplying up to 40 lb N/ac so growers can cut synthetic nitrogen by that amount.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Any response shows up at low to moderate N: in Illinois the gain appeared at 45–135 kg N/ha and vanished at 0 and 225 kg N/ha, and the fertilizer-N equivalent fell from 38.5 kg/ha at V8 to 11.5 kg/ha for grain yield.
  • Kentucky saw a significant gain in 2023 and, in 2024, only at 215 lb N/ac under heavy rye residue; the Kentucky authors say they cannot predict where or when a product will work.
  • Cutting N without an untreated reduced-N check can hide a loss: a 2022 Nebraska comparison at 106 lb N/ac plus PROVEN 40 yielded 195 bu/ac against 235 bu/ac for a 142 lb N/ac check.
  • Handling is strict: use within 24 hours of opening, non-chlorinated water, add it last, and avoid 7-21-7, ATS or UAN as a carrier.
  • Gene-edited bacteria are not OMRI-listed, so it is not an option for organic corn.
  • The manufacturer’s own 2024 Northern Plains strip trials (20 locations) reported −0.4 bu/ac with 38.5 lb/ac less N; that is company data, not independent.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Performance of Selected Commercially Available Asymbiotic N-fixing Products in the North Central Region ↗
    ExtensionFranzen et al., NCERA-103 · North Dakota State University Extension, SF2080 · 2023

    Land-grant trials in ten states through 2022: 59 of 61 corn site-years showed no yield increase from any product over the N rate alone. The review reported a significant yield benefit for PROVEN 40 in 0 of 15 site-years and Utrisha N in 0 of 11.

  2. Soil inoculation with nitrogen-fixing bacteria to supplement maize fertilizer need ↗
    Peer-reviewedWoodward, Sible, Seebauer & Below · Agronomy Journal 117(1), University of Illinois · 2025doi:10.1002/agj2.21729

    Four Illinois site-years (2019–21): the PROVEN 40 inoculant raised grain yield by an average 0.11 Mg/ha (about 1.8 bu/ac), with the gain at 45–135 kg N/ha and none at 0 or 225. The authors calculate a 2–6 bu/ac gain is needed to profit at $25–75/ha and conclude the effect did not warrant replacing fertilizer N.

  3. Biological N Fixation Products for Corn: An Update ↗
    ExtensionGrove & Lee · University of Kentucky Grain Crops · 2025

    Kentucky 2023: PROVEN 40 plots averaged 200 bu/ac at both 140 and 180 lb N against 199 and 191 without, a significant difference the authors report as 9 bu/ac; in 2024 it helped only at 215 lb N under heavy rye residue. Regionally, 2 positive site-years out of 61.

  4. Biological Nitrogen Fixation in Corn: Wisconsin Trial Results ↗
    University trialGeissinger & Marzu · UW–Madison Nutrient & Pest Management Program · 2024

    Five 2023 Wisconsin N-rate trials (0–280 lb N/ac) found neither corn grain yield nor the yield response to N was affected by PROVEN 40 at any site. The authors see no significant current evidence that a gene-edited N-fixer can meet corn N needs in Wisconsin.

  5. Genetic remodeling of soil diazotrophs enables partial replacement of synthetic nitrogen fertilizer with biological nitrogen fixation in maize ↗
    ManufacturerMartinez-Feria et al. (Pivot Bio, with UW–Madison and Purdue co-authors) · Scientific Reports 14; company-authored · 2024doi:10.1038/s41598-024-78243-3

    In 58 commercial fields (2021), cutting N by 35–40 lb/ac with PROVEN 40 produced no significant yield reduction, and isotope measurements estimated 21.2 kg N/ha from fixation by VT–R1, about 11% of plant N. This study was authored by the manufacturer with university co-authors.

Before you buy, ask

  1. Is the formulation and application method I would buy the one that was tested?
  2. Was the comparison a yield gain at the same nitrogen rate, or held yield with less fertilizer?
  3. What happens to my result if I cut nitrogen and the product does not fix enough?
