Corn foliar fungicide at VT/R1

Various suppliers (strobilurin, triazole and SDHI premixes) · Corn

Crop protectionConventionalVT/R1 timingTar spot and gray leaf spotMultiple modes of action

What the research measured

The cited 2014–15 meta-analysis across 13 states and Ontario reported a mean corn yield response of 332.9 kg/ha (about 5.3 bu/ac) to a foliar fungicide, with 68.2% of 436 treatment-studies positive and the VT timing 2.9 times the V6 response. The earlier 2002–09 meta-analysis reported mean gains of 230 to 390 kg/ha (about 3.7 to 6.2 bu/ac) by product. Under high tar spot severity in Indiana the average gain was 14.6 bu/A; under low severity it was −2.7 bu/A, and Iowa State detected no yield effect at any of six 2022 locations.

Two multi-state meta-analyses of uniform university trials (2002–09, 14 states; 2014–15, 13 states and Ontario), a Crop Protection Network summary of 2019–21 multi-state tar spot trials, Iowa State’s six-location 2022 program and a single 2024 Purdue planting-date trial.5 independent sources.

What it is. A single foliar pass of a strobilurin (QoI), triazole (DMI) or SDHI fungicide, usually a premix of two or three of them, sprayed at tassel to silking (VT/R1) to protect the ear leaf and canopy from tar spot, gray leaf spot and other late-season leaf diseases. Ground or aerial application; Trivapro at 13.7 fl oz/A was the product in the Indiana timing experiments cited here.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Response tracks disease: the Indiana tar spot study averaged +14.6 bu/A (range 6.2 to 22.2) under high severity and −2.7 bu/A (range −11.9 to 9.3) under low severity, where the partial net return ran from a $46.5 loss to a $5.8/A loss.
  • Iowa State’s 2022 trials at six research farms detected no effect of any fungicide on yield (P>0.1), even though V12 and R1 applications reduced tar spot and gray leaf spot; non-sprayed check yields ran from 178.6 to 250.5 bu/ac and tar spot reached 23.2% in the Crawfordsville check.
  • At low disease (<5% severity) the 2002–09 analysis put the probability of not recovering a $40–95/ha application at 0.55–0.98 for pyraclostrobin and 0.91–0.99 for azoxystrobin at $0.12/kg corn; above 5% severity the range was 0.25–0.69 for propiconazole + trifloxystrobin and 0.37–0.98 for azoxystrobin.
  • Timing matters: the 2014–15 analysis found VT (376.8 kg/ha) and V6 + VT (493.9 kg/ha) responses well above V6 alone (127.4 kg/ha), and an R5 application in Indiana gave the smallest tar spot reduction (13%).
  • Mode-of-action count matters: DMI + SDHI + QoI premixes averaged 574.4 kg/ha against 180.5 kg/ha for a solo QoI, DMI alone gave no substantial response, and the tar spot trials found two- or three-MOA products suppressed disease more than one.
  • The 7–18 bu/A protection in Purdue’s 2024 trial came from one northern Indiana site and was significant only at the earlier of two planting dates.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Meta-analysis of yield response of foliar fungicide-treated hybrid corn in the United States and Ontario, Canada ↗
    Meta-analysisWise, Smith, Freije, Mueller, Kandel, Allen et al. · PLoS ONE 14(6): e0217510 · 2019doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0217510

    Uniform trials in 13 states and Ontario in 2014–15: 68.2% of 436 treatment-studies had a positive yield response and the overall mean was 332.9 ± 29.1 kg/ha (about 5.3 bu/ac). VT applications (376.8 kg/ha) returned 2.9 times the V6 response (127.4 kg/ha); DMI + SDHI + QoI premixes gave 574.4 kg/ha, DMI + QoI 390.8 and QoI alone 180.5.

  2. Meta-analysis of yield response of hybrid field corn to foliar fungicides in the U.S. Corn Belt ↗
    Meta-analysisPaul, Madden, Bradley, Robertson, Munkvold, Shaner, Wise et al. · Phytopathology 101(9): 1122–1132 · 2011doi:10.1094/PHYTO-03-11-0091

    University trials in 14 states, 2002–09: mean yield gains of 390 kg/ha for propiconazole + trifloxystrobin, 331 for propiconazole + azoxystrobin, 256 for pyraclostrobin and 230 for azoxystrobin, all significant but highly variable. At $0.12/kg corn and $40–95/ha costs, the probability of not recovering the cost was 0.55–0.99 when disease severity was under 5%.

  3. Multi-state fungicide efficacy trials to manage tar spot and improve economic returns in corn in the U.S. and Canada ↗
    University trialGoodnight, Telenko, Ross, Chilvers, Allen, Ames et al. · Crop Protection Network, CPN-5015 · 2024doi:10.31274/cpn-20240904-0

    Small-plot trials in 2019–21 in Illinois, Indiana, Michigan, Wisconsin and Ontario: yield protection from a VT/R1 fungicide ranged from 1.5 to 7.9% under low to moderate tar spot and 3.1 to 8.5% under high severity. In the Indiana timing experiments (Wanatah and West Lafayette, Trivapro) the average gain was 14.6 bu/A (6.2–22.2) under high severity and −2.7 bu/A (−11.9 to 9.3) under low severity.

  4. 2022 Summary of Foliar Fungicide Applications on Corn in Iowa ↗
    University trialRobertson · Iowa State University Extension, Integrated Crop Management · 2023

    Six Iowa research farms, six replications, seven products at V12 and eight at R1: no effect of any fungicide on yield at any location (P>0.1). Non-sprayed check yields ran from 178.6 to 250.5 bu/ac; V12 and R1 applications reduced tar spot and gray leaf spot (P<0.1), while planting or V6 applications had little effect.

  5. Tar Spot: What To Look For In Corn And Making An Informed Decision On Fungicide Application ↗
    University trialTelenko · Purdue University Pest&Crop Newsletter, Issue 2025.15 · 2025

    In a single 2024 trial at the Pinney Purdue Ag Center in northern Indiana, a VT/R1 fungicide gave 7–18 bu/A yield protection over the nontreated and significantly raised yield only in the first of two planting dates; tar spot did not reach the ear leaf until July 25.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What disease pressure did the trials with a yield response have, and do my fields see it?
  2. What application timing and rate did the cited studies use?
  3. Is there a scouting threshold that would tell me whether to apply this season?
