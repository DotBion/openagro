Hairy vetch cover crop seed

Various seed suppliers · Before corn

Cover cropsLegumeConventional + organicWinter-hardy legumeAlso studied in rye mixes

What the research measured

Maryland trials reported an economic nitrogen rate of about 27 lb/ac after vetch versus 179 lb/ac without a cover crop. Pennsylvania measurements averaged 164 lb N/ac in vetch biomass at termination. These are different measures; the linked studies describe their locations, management, and sampling methods.

Two-site, two-year replicated Maryland kill-date trials, a two-site USDA-ARS mixture study, a 55 site-year meta-analysis and the legume-cover meta-analyses.5 independent sources.

What it is. A winter-hardy annual legume seeded in late summer or fall, alone or with rye, that fixes the largest spring N supply of the common winter legumes when killed at early bloom before no-till corn.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Kill date drives the credit: vetch added about 2 kg N/ha per day from late March to early May in Maryland; Wisconsin and Minnesota credit about 60 lb N/ac when incorporated before June 10 versus 120 for a full season.
  • N release is fast, about 70 lb N/ac within four weeks of corn planting in an Illinois study, which fits corn demand but can be lost in a wet spring; mixing with rye slows release and adds cover.
  • Winter survival: hardy to Zone 4 per SARE, but the Wisconsin and Minnesota bulletin says it often fails in the northern Midwest, and Nebraska stands measured only 12–29 lb N/ac.
  • Stand N is not fertilizer-replacement N: Extension credits range from 75–150 lb N/ac (Maryland) to 80–100 (North Carolina); at full N rates expect the return to be the fertilizer saved, not extra bushels.
  • Seeding: 15–20 lb/ac drilled or 25–30 broadcast; for maximum N in a mix the USDA-ARS study used 27 kg/ha vetch with 34 kg/ha rye.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Kill Date of Vetch, Rye, and a Vetch–Rye Mixture: II. Soil Moisture and Corn Yield ↗
    Peer-reviewedClark, Decker, Meisinger & McIntosh · Agronomy Journal 89, University of Maryland / USDA-ARS · 1997doi:10.2134/agronj1997.00021962008900030011x

    At the Maryland Coastal Plain site the economic N rate for no-till corn was 30 kg/ha after hairy vetch, 65 after a vetch–rye mix, 161 after rye and 201 with no cover; late-April or early-May kills consistently out-yielded earlier kills.

  2. Kill Date of Vetch, Rye, and a Vetch–Rye Mixture: I. Cover Crop and Corn Nitrogen ↗
    Peer-reviewedClark, Decker, Meisinger & McIntosh · Agronomy Journal 89 · 1997doi:10.2134/agronj1997.00021962008900030010x

    Between late March and early May hairy vetch added about 2 kg N/ha per day, reaching 144–203 kg N/ha in topgrowth; corn N requirement was lowest after vetch, intermediate after vetch–rye and highest after rye.

  3. Biomass Production and Nitrogen Accumulation by Hairy Vetch–Cereal Rye Mixtures: A Meta-Analysis ↗
    Meta-analysisThapa, Poffenbarger, Tully, Ackroyd, Kramer & Mirsky · Agronomy Journal 110, USDA-ARS · 2018doi:10.2134/agronj2017.09.0544

    Across 55 site-years from 21 US studies, vetch–rye mixtures produced 63% more biomass than vetch alone and held 150% more N than rye alone; with vetch above 46% of the seed mix by weight, the mixture accumulated as much N as the best monoculture.

  4. Biomass and Nitrogen Content of Hairy Vetch–Cereal Rye Cover Crop Mixtures as Influenced by Species Proportions ↗
    Peer-reviewedPoffenbarger et al. · Agronomy Journal 107, USDA-ARS Beltsville · 2015doi:10.2134/agronj14.0462

    At two Maryland sites, spring N content rose from 64 to 181 kg N/ha as the vetch share of biomass went from 0 to 100%, plateauing once vetch reached about half the stand; biological fixation supplied 63% of vetch N in monoculture and 86% in mixture.

  5. Hairy Vetch, in Managing Cover Crops Profitably (3rd edition) ↗
    GovernmentSARE (USDA-NIFA) · n.d.

    Maryland vetch–rye mixtures accumulated 130–180 lb N/ac, and late-April or early-May kills with corn no-tilled 10 days later consistently out-yielded earlier kills. Winter hardy through Zone 4; drill 15–20 lb/ac or broadcast 25–30.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What seeding rate, date, and termination timing did the cited studies use?
  2. How does termination timing before my cash crop change the nitrogen and moisture picture?
  3. Which species or mixture fits my rotation window and equipment?
