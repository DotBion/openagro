Crimson clover cover crop seed

Various seed suppliers · Before corn

Cover cropsLegumeForage use ↗Conventional + organicWinter-annual legumeOverwinters in Zones 6–8

What the research measured

Published Extension measurements describe roughly 50–100 lb N/ac in crimson clover biomass in some regions. Biomass nitrogen and fertilizer-equivalent nitrogen credit are different measures. Winter survival, planting date, and termination timing affect the reported results.

The meta-analyses cover legumes generally rather than crimson clover specifically, the multi-year peer-reviewed crimson-clover corn trial found is a single Ontario site, and the Midwest N-credit and winter-survival numbers come from Extension summaries.5 independent sources.

What it is. A winter-annual legume seeded in late summer or fall that fixes nitrogen and, where it overwinters, supplies part of the following corn crop’s N when killed in late spring.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Winter survival by region: dependable in Zone 8, survivable in the lower reaches of Zone 6, winter-killed in Zone 5 and colder; Michigan lists winter kill and seed cost as the main drawbacks.
  • Variety matters: in a one-year Illinois trial the older Dixie survived winter 69% versus 94% for Kentucky Pride, with 14 versus 187 lb N/ac in spring biomass. That write-up is by the seed company, so treat it as company data.
  • A winter-killed stand releases much of its N before corn can use it, so fall-only growth gives erosion and organic-matter benefits but little spring credit.
  • Kill late: N accumulates through spring, and the 2026 meta-analysis found termination under 7 days before planting roughly doubled the unfertilized-corn benefit versus early termination.
  • Ohio’s reliable establishment window is June 10 to September 28, which rules out seeding after most Corn Belt corn harvests; it needs true-clover rhizobium inoculant where clover has not been grown in three years.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Corn responses to nitrogen fertilization as influenced by cover cropping: a meta-analysis ↗
    Meta-analysisJuneja, Baath, Brar, Flynn, Yost & Rajan · Agronomy for Sustainable Development 46 · 2026doi:10.1007/s13593-025-01067-6

    Across 392 corn-yield observations from 55 site-years, legume cover crops raised corn yield 51% when no N was applied, a benefit that shrank as N rate rose; grass covers cut unfertilized corn yield 20%. Late termination gave a larger unfertilized-yield benefit than early termination.

  2. Corn yield response to winter cover crops: an updated meta-analysis ↗
    Meta-analysisMarcillo & Miguez · Journal of Soil and Water Conservation 72(3), Iowa State University · 2017doi:10.2489/jswc.72.3.226

    Across 268 observations from 65 studies (1965–2015), grass covers such as rye neither raised nor lowered corn yield on average. Legume covers raised corn yield 30–33% only where fertilizer N was low or the system was no-till.

  3. Legume Cover Crops Provide Nitrogen to Corn During a Three-Year Transition to Organic Cropping ↗
    Peer-reviewedYang, Drury, Reynolds & Reeb (Agriculture and Agri-Food Canada) · Agronomy Journal · 2019doi:10.2134/agronj2018.10.0652

    In a three-year southwestern Ontario rotation, summer-seeded crimson clover carried 119 kg N/ha at spring plow-down (hairy vetch 240, red clover 199). Corn after the legumes averaged 13.1 Mg/ha versus 6.4 for organic plots with no cover crop.

  4. Soil Organic Matter and Cover Crop-based Nitrogen Recommendations for Corn ↗
    ExtensionWhite · Penn State Extension · 2024

    From hundreds of Pennsylvania measurements, crimson clover biomass at spring termination averaged 79 lb N/ac (range 53–105), against 164 (134–194) for hairy vetch and 91 (45–137) for red clover. Penn State calls its cover-crop N recommendation system highly experimental.

  5. Crimson Clover, in Managing Cover Crops Profitably (3rd edition) ↗
    GovernmentSARE (USDA-NIFA) · n.d.

    As a winter annual in Zone 8 crimson clover fixes 70–150 lb N/ac by mid-May; a Michigan summer-annual stand made only 50–60 lb N/ac. It overwinters dependably in Zone 8, can survive the lower reaches of Zone 6, and winter-kills in Zone 5 and colder.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What seeding rate, date, and termination timing did the cited studies use?
  2. How does termination timing before my cash crop change the nitrogen and moisture picture?
  3. Which species or mixture fits my rotation window and equipment?
