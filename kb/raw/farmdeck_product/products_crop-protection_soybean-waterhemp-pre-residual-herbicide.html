Preemergence residual herbicide for waterhemp in soybean

Various suppliers (Group 14, 15 and 5 actives: flumioxazin, sulfentrazone, S-metolachlor, pyroxasulfone, metribuzin) · Soybean

Crop protectionConventionalPRE at plantingLayered residualGroups 5, 14, 15

What the research measured

The cited Iowa State summary of 2015–19 trials reported that pyroxasulfone alone never fell below 88% waterhemp control at three weeks, while S-metolachlor, dimethenamid-P and acetochlor gave 82% or more half the time but fell below 50% at worst and flumioxazin fell below 60% in one of five years. Purdue’s multi-year screening put Spartan, Valor, Outlook, Zidua and Warrant at 90% or greater at 28 days and metribuzin or Prowl in the mid-70s. In Minnesota’s 2015 trial a single PRE held 62 to 81% control by late September against 90 to 95% for a layered program, with a 10-bushel average yield difference.

Iowa State’s five-year PRE screening summary and 2021 program comparison, Purdue’s multi-year residual screening at 28 days, one replicated Minnesota layered-residual trial (2015) and one Ohio on-farm trial (2017). All are percent-control ratings; only Minnesota reports yield.5 independent sources.

What it is. A soil-applied residual herbicide, a Group 14 (flumioxazin, sulfentrazone), Group 15 (S-metolachlor, acetochlor, dimethenamid, pyroxasulfone) or Group 5 (metribuzin) active or a premix, sprayed at or just before soybean planting so waterhemp seedlings die as they germinate. Because waterhemp emerges from May into August, the PRE is usually followed by a postemergence pass that adds a second residual, the “layered” program.

Study conditions and limitations
  • Residual length is short for some actives: Purdue found metribuzin very active for two to three weeks, Prowl at 70 to 80% for four to six weeks and Sharpen at 1 oz/A good for about 10–14 days, and lists minimum rates for 4–5 weeks of control (at least 2 oz Valor SX, 6 oz Authority/Spartan 4F, 2 oz Zidua WG, 5–6 oz metribuzin 75 DF).
  • Emergence outlasts any PRE: Iowa State reports waterhemp emerging May through August at up to 1 to 1¼ inches a day, and in Ohio no soil-applied herbicide gave season-long control, so a postemergence pass was necessary.
  • Weather breaks residuals: in the Iowa 2015–19 data flumioxazin fell below 60% in one of five years and the Group 15 actives fell below 50% at their worst, with early breakdown in the wet spring of 2019.
  • Resistance narrows the options: almost 25% of 75 Iowa waterhemp populations collected in 2019 carried four-way resistance, and the Purdue screening population was ALS- and glyphosate-resistant with PPO resistance below 15%.
  • The yield evidence is one trial: Minnesota 2015 layered treatments yielded 46 to 51 bu/ac against 32 to 43 for PRE-only and 14 for the weedy check, under 51 waterhemp per square foot on June 8.
  • The Ohio trial was a single year, was not harvested, and planted June 2 after a May 16 PRE; its 70% to 99% August ratings include a Liberty pass in every treatment.
Read the sources · 5
  1. Performance of Preemergence Herbicides on Waterhemp Control in Soybean ↗
    University trialJha · Iowa State University Extension, Integrated Crop Management · 2020

    Five years (2015–19) of Iowa State herbicide evaluation trials with PRE products alone at labeled rates: pyroxasulfone’s lowest control was 88% at three weeks and it exceeded 95% half the time; S-metolachlor, dimethenamid-P and acetochlor gave 82% or more half the time but fell below 50% at worst; flumioxazin exceeded 95% half the time but fell below 60% in one of five years.

  2. Herbicide Programs for Waterhemp Control in Soybean ↗
    University trialJha & Yadav · Iowa State University Extension, Integrated Crop Management · 2021

    Comparing programs at 4 weeks after application, Verdict PRE at 5 oz/a gave 70% control, rising to 90% or more when followed by Liberty plus a Group 15 POST; two-pass programs with an effective PRE and POST showed no significant differences at 90% or better, while 2,4-D choline or glufosinate alone POST stayed below 90%. Almost 25% of 75 Iowa populations collected in 2019 had four-way resistance.

  3. Which Residual Herbicide Should I Use for Waterhemp Control in Soybeans? ↗
    University trialJohnson & Zimmer · Purdue University Pest&Crop Newsletter · 2023

    Pooling Purdue screening trials from several years (at least four observations per herbicide) on an ALS- and glyphosate-resistant population: Spartan, Valor, Outlook, Zidua and Warrant gave 90% or greater control at 28 days, Valor alone or in premixes 94% or higher, Group 15 premixes 93% or greater, 4 to 6 oz metribuzin alone and Prowl in the mid-70s, and 1 oz Sharpen under 60%.

  4. Got waterhemp? Layer residual herbicides to maintain control ↗
    University trialBehnken, Breitenbach, Gunsolus & Bongard · University of Minnesota Extension · 2021

    Rochester, Minnesota, 2015: by the end of September waterhemp control was 90 to 95 percent in the two-pass layered program against 62 to 81 percent for a single PRE (Group 15 products Dual II Magnum, Outlook and Warrant, each with Pursuit); layered plots yielded 46 to 51 bu/ac, PRE-only 32 to 43, the weedy check 14, a 10-bushel average difference.

  5. Management of Waterhemp in LibertyLink Soybeans ↗
    On-farm researchStachler · Ohio State University Extension, Auglaize County on-farm report · 2017

    One 2017 trial near St. Marys, Ohio, four replications: residual PREs (Valor XLT, Valor XLT + metribuzin, Fierce XLT) held 82 to 84% control on June 26 before the Liberty pass, and on August 15 control was 70% with no residual, 88% for Valor XLT, 92% for Fierce XLT and 99% for Valor XLT followed by Zidua POST.

Before you buy, ask

  1. What disease pressure did the trials with a yield response have, and do my fields see it?
  2. What application timing and rate did the cited studies use?
  3. Is there a scouting threshold that would tell me whether to apply this season?
