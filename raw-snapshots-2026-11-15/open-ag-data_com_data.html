Ground-truth data layers

Field evidence, tied to the ground it came from.

Agricultural research is scattered across thousands of studies, and almost none of it is tied to the place it happened. So nobody can say with confidence what works, where. We read the methods sections, resolve the site to a real county, and keep the sentence that proves it.

What one row gives you

One row is one observation — a place, a season, and something that was actually measured there. Not a paper, not an abstract.

Where
State, county, county FIPS, and a field coordinate when the study reported one
What practice
Tillage class, cover crop, crop, irrigation — normalized, not free text
What was measured
Soil carbon, yield, emissions and similar, with units and sampling depth
How we know
The verbatim sentence, the method used to place it, and a confidence level
Where it came from
The study title and DOI, so you can go read it yourself

Agencies & policy teams

Practice and outcome evidence at county grain, between the five-year census releases.

Every row quotes its source

Each observation carries the verbatim sentence it was read from, and the study it sits in. If we cannot show you the sentence, we do not sell you the row.

Counties resolve against real geometry

Every county matches the same polygons our maps render, so a FIPS code joins cleanly to yours. A location we cannot place is left unplaced rather than guessed.

Thin evidence stays quiet

Agreement rates are suppressed below five comparable observations, and a practice measured under a different definition is flagged, not silently compared.
