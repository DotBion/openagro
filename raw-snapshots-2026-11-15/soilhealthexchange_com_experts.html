Musfiq Salehin is Assistant Professor of Soil Science with Texas A&M AgriLife Research at the Stephenville Research and Extension Center. His research integrates soil fertility, nutrient management, and climate-smart practices to improve productivity and sustainability across semi-arid and subtropical cropping systems. The work focuses on agronomic management that improves nutrient- and water-use efficiency in row crops, spanning cover crops, organic cotton, sorghum, and irrigated systems and combining field trials with environmental sensing, eddy covariance, remote sensing, and machine learning. Musfiq holds a Ph.D. in Agronomy from Texas A&M University, an M.S. in Plant and Environmental Sciences from New Mexico State University, and a B.S. in Soil, Water, and Environment from the University of Dhaka. On Soil Health Exchange, he brings practical soil-fertility and nutrient-management expertise grounded in climate-smart cropping systems.

Dr. Jaya (J) Nepal is an early-career researcher with PhD in Soil and Water Sciences from University of Florida. Jonathan Kerman is a Soil Scientist and Agronomic Advisor at Ward Laboratories, where he helps growers, researchers, and industry partners translate complex biogeochemical data into clear, practical field decisions. His work connects laboratory science with applied agronomy, with particular emphasis on nutrient cycling, soil health assessment, water quality, and soil-plant-microbe interactions. Bryan Overstreet is a conservation agronomist with Purdue Extension and the Conservation Cropping Systems Initiative (CCSI), where he coordinates soil health work across northern Indiana. He works directly with farmers to put the four soil health principles to work on their own fields: keep living roots in the ground, minimize disturbance, keep the soil covered, and build diversity. His focus is cover crops and no-till. Raised on a farm near Waveland, Indiana, Bryan has worked with no-till farmers for more than 30 years. He began as an agronomist for a local agricultural cooperative, then spent nearly 18 years as Purdue Extension's agriculture and natural resources educator in Jasper County, where he led field days, private pesticide applicator recertification, and drone certification trainings. He moved into his conservation role in 2023. He also serves on the Purdue On The Farm leadership team, helping Extension educators scout crops and set up demonstration plots on working farms. Bryan was named Indiana Certified Crop Adviser of the Year in 2018 and received the National Association of County Agricultural Agents Distinguished Service Award in 2023. On Soil Health Exchange, he answers questions on cover crops, no-till, weed management, and soil conservation in Midwest row-crop systems.

Dr. Merilynn Schantz is a Rangeland Research Scientist at the USDA-ARS, Grassland, Soil and Water Research Laboratory in Temple, TX. Dr. Schantz’s research focuses on developing tools and assessing management strategies for advancing grassland science and management.
