Musfiq Salehin is Assistant Professor of Soil Science with Texas A&M AgriLife Research at the Stephenville Research and Extension Center.
His research integrates soil fertility, nutrient management, and climate-smart practices to improve productivity and sustainability across semi-arid and subtropical cropping systems. The work focuses on agronomic management that improves nutrient- and water-use efficiency in row crops, spanning cover crops, organic cotton, sorghum, and irrigated systems and combining field trials with environmental sensing, eddy covariance, remote sensing, and machine learning.
Musfiq holds a Ph.D. in Agronomy from Texas A&M University, an M.S. in Plant and Environmental Sciences from New Mexico State University, and a B.S. in Soil, Water, and Environment from the University of Dhaka. On Soil Health Exchange, he brings practical soil-fertility and nutrient-management expertise grounded in climate-smart cropping systems.

Dr. Jaya (J) Nepal is an early-career researcher with PhD in Soil and Water Sciences from University of Florida. His PhD work evaluated different biochar and carbon nanoparticles as organic amendments for field crop production in acidic-sandy soils of Florida with a research lens in soil, plant and environment fertility-health continuum. In his postdoctoral experiences, he has continued research in organic cereal production systems (NY, Cornell University) and currently in vegetable production system (PA, Penn State University) with main objective of understanding how agricultural management systems can be modified/designed with a “soil health” focus while balancing productivity. Jaya’s work particularly embraces the complexity and uniqueness of each agro-ecosystem and is interested in adapting the concept of soil health with a context-based approach. Jaya is particularly interested in how ecological tools such as cover crops, biochar, tillage practices can be evaluated and adapted to fit the specific goals of each farm.

Dr. Jed Grow is an Agronomist and Soil Scientist at AGVISE Laboratories — one of the largest soil testing labs in the Northern Plains, serving producers, crop consultants, and ag retailers across the United States and Canada. He holds a PhD in Plant Sciences from North Dakota State University along with CCA and 4R Nutrient Management Specialist certifications.
His path into agriculture started in Peru, where two years of service work exposed him to the realities of food insecurity and pointed him toward applied plant science. He returned to complete an undergraduate degree in genetics and biotechnology, working on quinoa variety breeding for a humanitarian project, and later earned his master's and doctorate at NDSU, with research focused on potato management and reducing losses from potato early die disease.
Outside of the lab, Jed is a devoted husband and father of four, a hockey and curling player, and a lifelong potato enthusiast — both scientifically and at the dinner table.
On Soil Health Exchange, Jed helps producers translate soil and plant tissue data into practical, field-tested management decisions.

Jonathan Kerman is a Soil Scientist and Agronomic Advisor at Ward Laboratories, where he helps growers, researchers, and industry partners translate complex biogeochemical data into clear, practical field decisions. His work connects laboratory science with applied agronomy, with particular emphasis on nutrient cycling, soil health assessment, water quality, and soil-plant-microbe interactions.
Kerman developed his research foundation at Texas A&M University, studying nutrient cycling and soil structure. Using X-ray computed tomography, he examined soil pore architecture and aggregate structure to understand how physical conditions shape water movement, nutrient availability, and microbial habitat. He brings that systems perspective to recommendations designed to be scientifically grounded and operationally realistic.

Bryan Overstreet is a conservation agronomist with Purdue Extension and the Conservation Cropping Systems Initiative (CCSI), where he coordinates soil health work across northern Indiana. He works directly with farmers to put the four soil health principles to work on their own fields: keep living roots in the ground, minimize disturbance, keep the soil covered, and build diversity. His focus is cover crops and no-till.
Raised on a farm near Waveland, Indiana, Bryan has worked with no-till farmers for more than 30 years. He began as an agronomist for a local agricultural cooperative, then spent nearly 18 years as Purdue Extension's agriculture and natural resources educator in Jasper County, where he led field days, private pesticide applicator recertification, and drone certification trainings. He moved into his conservation role in 2023. He also serves on the Purdue On The Farm leadership team, helping Extension educators scout crops and set up demonstration plots on working farms.
Bryan was named Indiana Certified Crop Adviser of the Year in 2018 and received the National Association of County Agricultural Agents Distinguished Service Award in 2023. On Soil Health Exchange, he answers questions on cover crops, no-till, weed management, and soil conservation in Midwest row-crop systems.

Hans Klopp is the SDSU Extension Soil Health Field Specialist, based at the Watertown Regional Center since 2023. He helps South Dakota producers build soil health through no-till, cover crops, residue management, and livestock integration. He leads Soil Health Hour, SDSU Extension’s monthly soil health webinar, and writes extension articles on topics from bulk density as a soil health indicator to leaving crop residue in place instead of fall tillage.
Hans holds a BS in water resources from the University of Wisconsin–Stevens Point, an MS in soil science from North Dakota State University, and a PhD in soil science from the University of Wisconsin–Madison. Madhusudhan Adhikari is a Research Assistant at Montana State University whose work bridges two distinct research arcs: nitrogen and cover crop management in Midwest row-crop systems, and precision weed management in Montana fallow fields. Dr. Merilynn Schantz is a Rangeland Research Scientist at the USDA-ARS, Grassland, Soil and Water Research Laboratory in Temple, TX. Dr. Schantz’s research focuses on developing tools and assessing management strategies for advancing grassland science and management. Her current research includes developing seasonal climate and plant production forecasting tools for agricultural management applications, assessing large scale grazing land management strategies on the spatiotemporal variability of ecosystem structure and function, and evaluating the utility of grazed forages for improving plant production, animal nutrition, and soil health. Other research areas include weather and climate modeling, precision management, rangeland restoration, invasive species, and ecological succession. Dr. Schantz is a member of three professional societies; the Society for Range Management, the Soil Water Conservation Society, and the Crop Science Society of America where she serves on multiple committees. Dr. Schantz is also currently serving as the Managing Editor for Rangeland Ecology and Management.
