One record serves the farmer, the water buyer, the insurer, the lender and the supply chain.

Inside the workspace

Every model reads one shared field state.

Public data and the farmer’s answers describe the field. A shared layer works out its water, nitrogen and yield, once for the field and once for its conventional twin. Six outcome models read that state, and economics reads the other five.

Inputs

public data, plus the farmer

Soil

USDA SSURGO survey, or SoilGrids where it is unavailable: texture by layer, organic matter, drainage, slope.

Climate

ERA5 reanalysis, 1991 to 2020: monthly rain, temperature, evaporative demand, growing degree days.

Management

Ten answers from the farmer: crop, tillage, cover, nitrogen, rotation, irrigation, tiles, years in practice.

Shared field state

computed for the field and its conventional twin

Water balance

Monthly: runoff, crop water use, irrigation, percolation below the roots.

Nitrogen budget

Annual: supply, crop removal, gas loss, cover-crop capture, leaching.

Attainable yield

Set by heat; limited by water and nitrogen.

The six models

Published methods, named plainly.

Each model is built from published equations and public data, and each is compared with the twin where a comparison makes sense. Where it does not, the score is the field’s own, and we say so.

Evidence tiers

Better evidence pays more.

Nothing is paid until the practice itself is verified from records, invoices or imagery. A confidence factor then sets the share of a modeled outcome that is paid, and it rises as soil samples and well data come in. From what is paid, 10% goes to a risk reserve and up to 10% to verification.

Tier 1

60%

Modeled

Practice verified by records, invoices or imagery; outcome from process models.

Tier 2

80%

Modeled + sampled

Models calibrated with stratified soil and water samples.

Tier 3

95%

Measured

Edge-of-field monitoring and repeat soil sampling.

Standards

Trust is a product feature.

Environmental estimates become inputs to operating and financial decisions. So the platform shows its work, and says plainly what it cannot do yet.

What the models cannot do yet

  • These are screening models. They run monthly, on one point per field, for one typical year.
  • They do not model aquifer decline, district allocations, or the years it takes nitrate to reach a well.
  • They do not produce verified credits. Verification is a later step, with a buyer and an auditor.
  • They have not yet been checked against measured outcomes on Nebraska fields. That is what the pilot is for.
