Company

The evidence layer for an ecosystem dividend.

Farms filter water, store carbon, hold water in the soil and buffer weather. Those outcomes are real, but nobody keeps the books. TerraValue measures them field by field, prices them at what someone already pays, and records who paid, so the people who manage the land can be paid for what it produces.

Our mission

Make stewardship visible, comparable and paid for.

“The people managing the land should be able to understand, defend and benefit from the full value their decisions create.”

A town paying to strip nitrate from its wells, a food company buying carbon in its supply shed, an insurer discounting cover-cropped acres, a bank lending against a stewardship plan: each already pays for something a farm produces. They pay separately, for practices rather than outcomes, and the farmer sees only a fraction.

We are building the piece that is missing between them: a field-level estimate of what was produced, a price for each outcome at what the buyer avoids, and a ledger that lets several buyers pay for the same acre without paying for the same outcome twice. With those in one place, risk and revenue can be shared differently: the insurer prices the risk, the buyer pays for the outcome, the lender finances the gap, and the money reaches the farmer.

How TerraValue works

One loop, and the dividend pays for it.

Farmers come in, records prove what they did, the models estimate what it produced, public data checks the estimate, the ledger sells each outcome once, and the dividend brings the next farmer in.

  1. 1

    Find the field

    A farmer arrives through Soil Health Exchange, a district program, a lender or a farm manager, and marks the field on a map.

  2. 2

    Resolve it

    The boundary becomes a soil series, its layers, slope and drainage, and thirty years of climate, from USDA SSURGO and ERA5.

  3. 3

    Prove the practice

    Planting dates, tillage, nitrogen and cover-crop seed are proven by records and invoices, or by the forms a district already requires.

  4. 4

    Model the outcome

    A water balance, a nitrogen budget and six outcome models run on the field as farmed and on its conventional twin.

  5. 5

    Raise the evidence

    Deep soil samples, well readings, flowmeters and satellite water use check the estimates and lift the share that is paid.

  6. 6

    Price it

    Each outcome is valued at what a named buyer avoids or already pays: a treatment plant, a compliance cost, a credit.

  7. 7

    Record and pay

    Each unit gets one owner. The fund pays the dividend, observations recalibrate the model, and the neighbours see it.

How it all connects

One record, six buyers.

The dots connect at the field record. One season’s record of a nitrogen cut does six jobs, and each buyer pays for its own use. No single buyer would pay for the whole record. Together they pay for it several times over.

  1. 1It proves the practice to the natural resources district for a state nitrogen payment.
  2. 2It scores the bushels for the ethanol plant’s clean fuel credit.
  3. 3It documents the pounds of nitrate kept out of the town’s water.
  4. 4It sits in the lender’s file as the reason the loan will be repaid.
  5. 5It prices the insurer’s discount on the acres it covers.
  6. 6It shows the landowner what the lease is worth with the practice kept.

The evidence loop

Shared data raises the evidence tier, and the dividend on the same acre rises with it.

The adoption loop

A dividend paid on time is visible to neighbours. More enrolled fields in one source area make the change measurable in the town’s wells, which raises the tier for everyone there.

The capital loop

Buyer payments repay the lender. A repaid facility lets the lender fund the next cohort at lower risk.

Where the dots do not connect yet

  • No contracted anchor buyer yet. Until one town or district signs, every other buyer asks who it is.
  • No ledger yet. Without it, buyers protect themselves with exclusivity.
  • No district data feed yet. Without it, fields stay at the modeled tier.
  • No grazing models yet. The largest acreage in the state is not priced.

The big picture

From one town’s contract to an ecosystem marketplace.

The dividend is a payment. The marketplace is the institution that makes the payment routine: standard units, evidence grades, posted prices and a registry, with capital and rules as the rails. It is built one source-water area at a time, and each stage has to pay for itself.

Supply side

Who produces the outcome

  • Stewards

    Operators, landowners, ranchers, tribal and public landholders. They change the land and sell the outcome.

  • Evidence

    Records, invoices, district wells, soil samples, satellites. They prove the practice and grade the outcome.

  • Carriers

    Co-ops, agronomists, aggregators, nonprofits. They bring fields in and are paid per field verified.

The exchange

The ecosystem marketplace

Not a credit registry and not a cost-share program. A ledger where a field’s outcomes are standard units, graded by evidence, priced by who avoids what, and owned once.

  • Standard units

    Pounds of nitrate kept out. Tonnes of CO₂e. Acre-feet not pumped. Runoff retained. Habitat acres. A resilience score.

  • Grades

    Modeled, sampled, measured. The share paid rises with the grade, so better evidence is worth money.

  • Prices

    Posted per unit, anchored to what a named buyer avoids. The same acre can carry several lines.

  • Registry

    One owner per unit. Stacking without double counting, and no need for exclusivity.

Capital rail

Revolving funds, outcome funds, banks and investors carry the gap between planting and verification, and are repaid from unit sales.

Rules rail

State statutes, district rules, USDA’s carbon-intensity framework, registries and verifiers set the baseline, the unit and who may claim it.

Demand side

Who pays for the outcome

  • Public water and land

    Cities, utilities, districts, state and federal programs, irrigation and flood districts, downstream basins.

  • Finance and risk

    Lenders, crop insurers, reinsurers, Farmer Mac, impact investors, foundations, farmland investors.

  • Supply chain and brands

    Processors, food brands, ethanol plants, retailers, livestock integrators, input suppliers, bottlers.

  • Corporate water and climate

    Data centers, point-source dischargers, carbon buyers and registries.

How it gets built, in order

Each stage pays for itself before the next begins

  1. 1

    One source-water area

    A town or district with a nitrate bill signs the first outcome contract. Farmers in its wellhead area get free field reports. The district’s wells and samples set the evidence tier.

    Who
    City, NRD, farmers, Soil Health Exchange
    Unit traded
    Pounds of nitrate kept out
  2. 2

    Stack buyers on one ledger

    A grain or fuel buyer adds the carbon and carbon-intensity line, an insurer adds a discount, a lender finances the gap. The rule that makes it work: one owner per unit, no exclusivity.

    Who
    Processors, ethanol plants, insurers, Farm Credit
    Unit traded
    Tonnes of CO₂e, resilience, acre-feet
  3. 3

    A district and state exchange

    Units are standardized, evidence tiers become the grade, prices are posted, and a clearinghouse matches buyers and sellers across districts. State revolving funds and the Corn Board lend and co-fund.

    Who
    State agencies, SRFs, checkoffs, clearinghouse
    Unit traded
    Standard units at posted prices
  4. 4

    A basin-wide marketplace

    Downstream basins, reinsurers and the secondary loan market buy at scale. The dividend is paid every season from a pooled fund, and the ledger runs as public infrastructure.

    Who
    Basin compacts, reinsurers, Farmer Mac, foundations
    Unit traded
    A dividend per acre per season

How we build

Principles before features.

01

Farmer utility first

A model matters only if it improves an actual decision on an actual farm. The field report is free for that reason.

02

Evidence with lineage

Every output shows where the data came from, which method was used, and what remains uncertain.

03

Economics in the same frame

Environmental outcomes sit beside transition cost, operating risk and return, never in a separate report.

04

Say what it cannot do

Screening models, not verified credits. Root-zone estimates, not well readings. We say so before anyone relies on a number.

The path forward

Build credibility in the right order.

Now

01Field workspace and working paper

Six models on USDA soil and ERA5 climate for any US field, a published working paper on the ecosystem dividend, and the 45Z tool.

Next

02One source-water pilot

A town or natural resources district and the farmers in its wellhead area. Their wells, flowmeters and soil samples calibrate the models and raise the evidence tier.

Then

03The ledger and the first contracts

Each outcome owned by one buyer; outcome purchase agreements with a utility or district; supply-shed scoring for a grain or fuel buyer in the same area; a lender financing the gap.

Long term

04A marketplace across districts

Standard units at posted prices, a clearinghouse, basin-wide buyers, and a dividend that pays stewards every season from a pooled fund.
