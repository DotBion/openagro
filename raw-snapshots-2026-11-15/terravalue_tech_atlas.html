How to read it

Three rules every layer follows.

  1. 01

    Published data only

    Every layer is a dataset someone else published and stands behind. TerraValue reduces it to one number per county and changes nothing else. No layer is a TerraValue estimate.

  2. 02

    The date is part of the number

    Each layer says what year it describes and how often its publisher updates it. Where the newest figure is years old, the map says so before it shows it.

  3. 03

    A county is not a field

    A county average hides the fields inside it, and two neighbours on different soil can sit at opposite ends of the same class. Closing that gap is what the field models are for.

From the county to the field

The map shows where the problem is. A field shows what fixing it is worth.

TerraValue runs one field on its own surveyed soil and thirty years of its weather, as it is farmed and as it would be farmed conventionally, and prices the difference at what someone already pays.

A farm produces more than yield.

TerraValue measures what a field produces beyond the crop, prices it at what someone already pays, and records who paid, so the money can reach the people who manage the land.
