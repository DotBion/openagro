The platform
What a field produced, what it is worth, and who paid.
TerraValue runs a field the way it is farmed and the way it would be farmed conventionally, on the same soil and weather. The difference is an outcome someone can pay for: bushels, water kept, nitrate kept out, carbon, and dollars by who pays. One record serves the farmer, the water buyer, the insurer, the lender and the supply chain.

Inside the workspace
Every model reads one shared field state.
Public data and the farmer’s answers describe the field. A shared layer works out its water, nitrogen and yield, once for the field and once for its conventional twin. Six outcome models read that state, and economics reads the other five.

The six models
Published methods, named plainly.
Each model is built from published equations and public data, and each is compared with the twin where a comparison makes sense. Where it does not, the score is the field’s own, and we say so.
Model
What it computes
Method
Compared with
Soil carbon
Carbon gained or lost in the top 30 cm over ten years; nitrous oxide avoided when less nitrogen is applied.
A monthly pool model after RothC, with inputs from residue, roots, cover crops and manure; IPCC emission factors for nitrous oxide.
Carbon is the field’s own change. Nitrous oxide is against the twin.
Water quality
Soil lost to water erosion, nitrate leaving the root zone, phosphorus runoff risk.
The Revised Universal Soil Loss Equation; the shared nitrogen budget; a phosphorus index.
Against the twin, and priced at the dividend’s base water price.
Water quantity
Rain captured, irrigation pumped and what it costs, a drought-resilience index.
A monthly soil-water balance: Saxton and Rawls soil water, NRCS curve-number runoff, FAO-56 crop coefficients.
Irrigation against the twin; the rest is the field’s own.
Biodiversity
Habitat quality, pollinator support, soil biology and species diversity, each 0 to 100.
Practice indices after InVEST habitat quality and the NRCS pollinator habitat assessment.
An absolute score. Its dollar figure is shown but never counted.
Yield
Attainable yield for the climate, what water and nitrogen cost it, and dry-year retention.
A growing-degree-day curve set from USDA yields and trials; FAO-33 water response; a nitrogen response curve.
Against the twin, which sets the nitrogen yield penalty.
Economics
Ten lines of money per acre: inputs, erosion, pumping, carbon, water, cost-share, margins, transition.
USDA ARMS cost budgets, NRCS payment schedules, and prices already paid in the US.
Every line against the twin. Program lines are potential, not guaranteed.

Standards
Trust is a product feature.
Environmental estimates become inputs to operating and financial decisions. So the platform shows its work, and says plainly what it cannot do yet.
What the models cannot do yet
These are screening models. They run monthly, on one point per field, for one typical year.
They do not model aquifer decline, district allocations, or the years it takes nitrate to reach a well.
They do not produce verified credits. Verification is a later step, with a buyer and an auditor.
They have not yet been checked against measured outcomes on Nebraska fields. That is what the pilot is for.
