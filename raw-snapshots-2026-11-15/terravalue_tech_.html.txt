How it works
Same field, two ways. The difference is what your practices are worth.
Every result is a comparison: the field as you farm it against the same soil, slope and climate under conventional tillage, no cover crop and synthetic nitrogen at the yield-goal rate.
01
Resolve the field
A pin or a drawn boundary is looked up in the USDA soil survey and thirty years of climate: texture, organic matter, drainage, slope, rain, heat.

Add how it is farmed
Ten answers from the farmer: crop, tillage, cover crop, nitrogen rate, rotation, irrigation, tiles, years in practice.

03
Run it two ways
A monthly water balance, a nitrogen budget and six outcome models run on the field as farmed, and again on the same field farmed conventionally.

04
Price it and keep the books
Each difference is priced at what a named buyer already pays. Private savings, program money and costs are never mixed.

Three kinds of money
Your money, other people’s money, and what it costs.
Private savings land on the operating budget whether or not anyone pays. Program and market money is contingent on a buyer. A lender treats them differently, so the model never mixes them.

Why now
The value is counted in trillions. The payments are not.
Three published figures frame the gap: what nature’s work is worth, what it costs when a field loses nitrogen, and how little private money pays for either. Each is an estimate with a wide range, and each links to its source.
$125T/yr
estimated value of the world’s ecosystem services in 2011
In 2007 dollars, across every biome, against a world economy of about $75 trillion that year. The same study put the yearly loss from land-use change at $4.3 to $20.2 trillion.
Costanza et al. 2014, Global Environmental Change
$210B/yr
health and environmental damage from nitrogen released in the United States, early 2000s
The range is $81 to $441 billion a year, in 2008 dollars. This is the bill a pound of nitrate kept in the field is priced against.
Sobota et al. 2015, Environmental Research Letters
$571B/yr
nature-based solutions investment needed by 2030
UNEP says private finance contributed only $23 billion. The money and the buyers exist. What is missing is a way to measure an outcome, price it and pay the person who produced it.
