Why we exist

The evidence already exists. It's just not findable by the one thing that matters most — where the work was done.

A cover-crop trial in Nebraska shouldn't be buried next to one from a climate it has nothing in common with. We turn the agricultural research record into a map you can search by place and topic — so the next decision, the next RFA, the next field plan starts from evidence that actually applies.

What OpenAgData provides

Three reads on the evidence base.

01 / Mapped

Every study, pinned to place.

We geolocate field trials and meta-analyses to the soils, climates, and counties where they ran — so evidence from across the road counts more than evidence across the world.

02 / Measured

How the data flows

From scattered papers to practical answers.

Step 1

Papers indexed

Published studies, preprints, and reports are cataloged with a link back to the source of record — we index, we don't publish.

Step 2

Enriched with context

Each study is pinned to its state and county, then tagged with funders, topics, and the outcomes it measured.

Step 3

Organized into hubs & topics

Records are grouped into research hubs and topic collections, so evidence reads as a map instead of a list.

Step 4

Used for real decisions

Funders, researchers, and institutions search by place and topic to find evidence they can actually use.

From the founder
“A trial in Nebraska shouldn't be buried next to one from a climate it has nothing in common with.”

I'm a soil scientist. For years I watched the same thing happen: the evidence researchers and funders need already exists — it's just scattered across thousands of papers, none of them findable by the one thing that matters most: where the work was done.

So a decision-maker in the Mississippi Delta reads a meta-analysis built mostly on Midwestern soils, and a brilliant local trial sits uncited two counties over. The knowledge is there. The map isn't.

OpenAgData is my attempt to build that map — to turn the agricultural research record into something you can search by place and topic, so the next field plan, the next grant, the next policy starts from evidence that actually applies. We're early, and building in the open. If that resonates, I'd genuinely love to hear from you.
